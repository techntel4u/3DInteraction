Installing an AMD Threadripper CPU

Transcript of the narration · Level 3
▶ Open the simulation

Everything the narrator says, in order. Use it if you can't use sound, or to revise afterwards.

Step by step

1. Prepare safely

Switch off and unplug the mains cable from the power supply. Work on an antistatic mat, wear an ESD wrist strap connected to ground, and handle parts by their edges.

Common mistake: Skipping the wrist strap: a static discharge too small to feel can still damage the CPU or motherboard.

2. Loosen screws 3 → 2 → 1

Use a Torx T20 driver to loosen the three captive force-frame screws in reverse order: 3, then 2, then 1. The numbers are printed on the force frame.

Common mistake: Using the wrong bit size can strip the Torx heads. Use a T20.

3. Force frame springs open

The force frame is spring-loaded, so it pops up as the last screw is released. Lift it to the fully open position.

Common mistake: Keep fingers, tools and loose items away from the socket area while it is open.

4. Lift the rail frame

Pull up the tabs on both sides of the rail frame to release it, then lift it to its upright position. It is spring-loaded too, so keep hold of it.

Common mistake: Letting the rail frame snap up or down can knock the socket. Move it gently.

5. Remove the external cap

Slide the clear external cap up and out of the rail frame. Keep it: it goes back in if the board is ever stored or returned without a CPU.

Common mistake: The external cap is not the socket cover. The PnP cover cap on the socket stays on for now.

6. Slide in the carrier and CPU

Hold the orange carrier frame by its tab and slide it down the rails until it clicks into place. It only fits one way: the tab faces up and away from the socket.

Common mistake: Never take the CPU out of the orange carrier frame, and never force it into the rails.

7. Remove the PnP cover cap

Now lift the PnP cover cap off the socket using its tabs. The exposed socket contacts are extremely fragile: don’t touch them or let anything drop onto them.

Common mistake: Removing the cap too early leaves the contacts exposed for longer. On this socket the contacts are in the motherboard, so bent contacts usually mean a damaged board.

8. Close both frames

Lower the rail frame gently until it latches onto the socket housing, then lower the force frame over it.

Common mistake: Never press down on the CPU with your fingers to seat it. The force frame does the clamping.

9. Tighten screws 1 → 2 → 3

Start all three screws in the order 1, 2, 3, then tighten them fully in the same order with a torque-limiting T20 driver. Stop at the click: about 1.6 N·m (14 lbf·in). Check your motherboard manual.

Common mistake: Tightening in the wrong order, or over-tightening, can cause serious damage to the socket or the CPU.

10. Apply thermal paste

If the cooler has paste pre-applied, don’t add more. Otherwise apply paste as the cooler or paste maker’s guide shows. This large lid needs a pattern that spreads across the whole surface, not one small dot.

Common mistake: Too much paste lowers heat conductivity and cooling performance.

11. Fit the cooler

Remove the protective film from the cooler base, lower the cooler straight down onto the CPU, then tighten its screws evenly in a diagonal pattern, a little at a time. Use the cooler’s own instructions and torque, not the socket value.

Common mistake: Leaving the protective film on the cooler base blocks heat transfer, so the CPU can overheat.

12. Connect CPU_FAN

Plug the cooler fan into the CPU_FAN header on the motherboard so the board can read its speed and control it. Then reconnect power and check the CPU temperature and fan speed in the BIOS/UEFI.

Common mistake: Forgetting to connect the fan, or using the wrong header, means the board can’t properly monitor or control the CPU fan, and the CPU can overheat.
