Guided tour

1. Meet the brick

A standard UK brick is 215 mm long, 102.5 mm wide and 65 mm high. The long side is the stretcher face. The short end is the header face. The top and bottom are the bed faces.

2. Why we bond

Here every course is laid the same way, so the joints line up. The red lines show these straight joints. A wall like this is weak along those lines and can crack there.

3. Course 1

Stretcher bond is half a brick thick. Lay stretchers end to end with a 10 mm joint between them. One brick plus one joint measures 225 mm.

4. Turning the corner

At the corner, the quoin brick swaps direction every course. Its end shows as a header on the return wall. That starts the half lap on the return.

5. Half lap

Each brick sits across two bricks below, with its joint halfway along. This is a half lap. The straight joints check is on and there are no red lines.

6. Stopped end

At a stopped end, every other course finishes with a half bat. A half bat is a brick cut in half across its length. It keeps the half lap right to the end.

7. Keep to gauge

Each course is 75 mm: a 65 mm brick plus a 10 mm bed joint. Four courses measure 300 mm. Check the height against the gauge rod as you build.

8. Spot the fault

This wall has a fault. Tap a brick in the course where the mistake starts. Need a clue? Turn on Straight joints.

Common mix-ups

1. Mix-up: A small straight joint does not matter.

Right idea: Any joint that lines up with the joint below weakens the wall. Keep the half lap every course.

2. Mix-up: The corner brick goes the same way every course.

Right idea: The quoin swaps direction every course. That is what ties the two walls together.

3. Mix-up: A half bat and a queen closer are the same thing.

Right idea: A half bat is cut across the brick. A queen closer is cut down its length. Stretcher bond uses half bats.

4. Mix-up: Course height does not matter if the wall is level.

Right idea: Keep to 75 mm gauge per course so the wall matches the gauge rod and the drawing.
