Guided tour

1. What is a cavity wall?

A cavity wall is two walls, called leaves, with a gap between them called the cavity. The outer leaf is brick and the inner leaf is block. Wall ties hold the two leaves together. This is a short length of a longer wall, part-built.

2. Damp-proof course (DPC)

Below ground is the substructure. The damp-proof course (DPC) is a waterproof strip laid in a bed joint (horizontal mortar joint) of both leaves. It goes at least 150 mm above the outside ground, so damp from the ground can’t rise up the wall.

3. Inner leaf

The inner leaf (blockwork) goes up first. It is called the leading leaf. A concrete lintel spans the window opening. On site the leaves go up in lifts, a bit at a time — here each one is shown going up in one go so it’s easy to see.

4. Wall ties

Wall ties join the two leaves. In the main wall they go no more than 900 mm apart across and 450 mm apart up, staggered. Beside an opening they go within 225 mm of the edge, no more than 300 mm apart up. They are bedded in mortar as each lift is built, and kept off the DPC.

5. Insulation

Partial-fill insulation boards are fixed tight against the inner leaf. Retaining clips on the wall ties hold them in place. At least 50 mm of clear cavity is left between the boards and the outer leaf.

6. Outer leaf

The outer leaf (facing brickwork) goes up. Each tie sits at least 50 mm into each leaf, level or falling slightly outwards, with its drip in the middle of the clear cavity, pointing down. Keep mortar out of the cavity.

7. Openings

At the window, cavity closers close the cavity at the reveals (the sides of the opening). Above the lintel, a cavity tray with stop ends catches water in the cavity and runs it outwards. Weep holes let it out — at least two per opening, no more than 450 mm apart.

8. Keeping water out

Here the wall is cut through. Rain that soaks through the outer leaf runs down its inside face. It drips off the tie drips and drains away below the DPC — the cavity carries on at least 225 mm below it. Above the window, the tray and weep holes send it outside. The inner leaf stays dry.

9. When it goes wrong

Mortar droppings on ties or at the bottom of the cavity, ties sloping inwards, or a loose insulation board all make a bridge. Water crosses the cavity and causes damp inside. Now try the Spot the faults challenge.
