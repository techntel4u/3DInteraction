Tasks

  1. Guided tour: follow all 6 stages.

  6. Test yourself: do the matching task and the 6 questions.

Test yourself

Circle one answer for each question, then check your answers in the simulation.

1. Match the image to the tissue: on the CT slice in the simulation, which tissue is at each of points A, B, C and D? (Each answer is used once.)
  1. Air-filled lung (about −700 HU)
  2. Fat (about −100 HU)
  3. Soft tissue / blood (about +40 HU)
  4. Bone (+400 HU or more)
2. How does an X-ray tube produce X-rays?
  1. Electrons from a heated cathode are accelerated into a tungsten anode
  2. A radioactive source inside the tube decays
  3. Light from the filament is filtered into X-rays
  4. The spinning anode creates a magnetic field that emits X-rays
3. On a projection X-ray image, why does the spine appear white?
  1. Bone reflects X-rays back towards the tube
  2. Bone attenuates (absorbs) more X-rays, so fewer reach the detector
  3. Bone gives out its own X-rays
  4. The detector is more sensitive behind bone
4. How does a CT scanner build up a cross-sectional image?
  1. It takes one X-ray photo and cuts it into strips
  2. Detectors measure radiation given out by the patient’s organs
  3. It measures attenuation from many angles as the tube rotates, then a computer reconstructs the slice
  4. It uses sound echoes from the rotating gantry
5. A region on a CT slice reads −700 HU. Which tissue is it most likely to be?
  1. Bone
  2. Liver
  3. Lung
  4. Water
6. Using UKHSA typical doses, a chest CT (6.6 mSv) is roughly how many times a chest X-ray (0.014 mSv)?
  1. About 5 times
  2. About 50 times
  3. About 470 times
  4. About 4,700 times
7. A radiographer moves from 1 m to 2 m from the source of scatter. What happens to the scatter dose rate?
  1. It halves
  2. It falls to a quarter
  3. It stays the same
  4. It doubles
