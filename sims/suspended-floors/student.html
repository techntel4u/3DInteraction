  • describe a beam-and-block floor: precast beams on a DPC, infill blocks, insulation and screed
  • explain why sleeper walls are honeycombed and why airbricks go in opposite walls
  • state the minimum air gaps: 75 mm under wall plates and 150 mm under joists or beams
  • spot the building faults that lead to damp and rot in a suspended floor

Common misconceptions

Wrong: airbricks are there to ventilate the room.
Right: they ventilate the void under the floor, so the timbers stay dry.
Wrong: blocking an airbrick just stops a draught and does no harm.
Right: it stops the air moving through the void. Damp builds up and the timber can rot.
Wrong: sleeper walls are solid like any other wall.
Right: they are honeycombed, with gaps in the brickwork so air can pass through.
Wrong: a concrete beam-and-block floor doesn't need a ventilated void.
Right: it still needs a void of at least 150 mm, ventilated from opposite sides.
Wrong: the DPC only goes in the outside walls.
Right: there must be a DPC between timber and any masonry that can carry damp from the ground, including the top of sleeper walls.

This simulation covers 14.1. Airbricks and the ventilated void aren't named in the range, but they explain why the components are built the way they are. Floors make up 4 of the 40 questions in the Unit 202 multiple-choice test.
