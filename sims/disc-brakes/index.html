Learning outcomes

  • State the job of a braking system (to slow and stop the vehicle).
  • Identify the main parts: pedal, master cylinder, reservoir, pipes, caliper, piston, seal, pads, disc, slide pins.
  • Describe in simple steps how pressing the pedal makes the pads squeeze the disc.
  • Explain that friction turns movement into heat.
  • Recognise the signs of worn pads and air in the system.

Misconceptions to tackle

  • "The pedal pulls a cable to the pads." It pushes fluid.
  • "Fluid flows round and round." It barely moves; pressure carries the push.
  • "Only one pad moves." The piston pushes one pad; the caliper slides to pull the other.
  • "A spongy pedal means worn pads." Usually air in the fluid.

Questions to ask the class

  • Why must brake fluid not contain air?
  • Why does the disc get hot? Where does the energy come from?
  • What would happen if a slide pin seized?
  • Why does the fluid level drop as pads wear?

Safety reminders

  • Wear PPE. Brake fluid harms skin and eyes and strips paint.
  • Never blow brake dust with an air line.
  • Always check manufacturer data for minimum pad and disc thickness.
