Guided tour

1. Welcome

This is the disc brake from one wheel of a car, with the brake pedal and master cylinder. Some parts are cut in half so you can see inside. Drag to turn it. Pinch or scroll to zoom.

2. 1. Press the pedal

When you press the brake pedal, it pushes a rod into the master cylinder. The pedal works like a lever, so a small push from your foot becomes a big push.

3. 2. Brake fluid carries the push

The master cylinder pushes brake fluid (orange) along the pipe. A liquid cannot be squashed, so the push at the pedal comes out just as strong at the wheel. The arrows show the pressure travelling to the caliper.

4. 3. The piston pushes the pads

The fluid pushes the caliper piston out. The piston pushes the inner pad onto the disc. The caliper then slides on its pins and pulls the outer pad onto the other side.

5. 4. Friction slows the car

The pads rub on the spinning disc. This friction slows the wheel. The movement of the car turns into heat. Hard braking from high speed can make the disc glow red hot.

6. 5. Let go of the pedal

When you let go, the fluid pressure drops. The rubber piston seal pulls the piston back a little, so the pads stop touching the disc and the wheel can turn freely.

7. Fault: worn brake pads

These pads are worn thin. The piston has to come out further, so the pedal goes down further. Thin pads grip less, so the car takes longer to stop. Listen for the squeal of the wear indicator.

8. Fault: air in the brake line

There is air (white bubbles) in the brake fluid. Air can be squashed, so the pedal feels spongy and sinks towards the floor. The brakes are much weaker. The fix is to bleed the brakes at the bleed nipple.

9. Inspect the parts

The brake is now taken apart. Tap or click any part to see what it does and what to check. Look at the pads, the disc, the slide pins and the piston seal.

What happens when you brake

1. 1. Press the pedal

Step one. Your foot pushes the brake pedal. The pedal pushes a rod into the master cylinder.

2. 2. Fluid carries the push

Step two. The master cylinder pushes brake fluid along the pipe. Fluid cannot be squashed, so it carries the push to the wheel.

3. 3. The pads squeeze the disc

Step three. The fluid pushes the caliper piston out. The piston pushes the inner pad onto the disc, and the caliper slides to pull the outer pad on too.

4. 4. Friction slows the car

Step four. The pads rub on the spinning disc. Friction slows the car, and the movement turns into heat.

5. 5. Let go of the pedal

Step five. You let go. The pressure drops, and the piston seal pulls the piston back a little, so the pads let go of the disc.

The parts

Brake pedal

The driver presses it with their foot. It works like a lever to make the push bigger. What to check: The pedal should feel firm and stop well above the floor.

Master cylinder

Turns the push from the pedal into brake fluid pressure. What to check: No fluid leaks around the master cylinder.

Brake fluid reservoir

Holds spare brake fluid for the system. What to check: Fluid level between MIN and MAX. A low level can mean worn pads or a leak.

Brake pipe and hose

Carry the brake fluid from the master cylinder to the caliper. What to check: No leaks, rust or damage. Flexible hoses must not be cracked or bulging.

Caliper

Holds the piston and pads. It slides on its pins so both pads squeeze the disc. What to check: No fluid leaks. The caliper should slide freely.

Caliper piston

Brake fluid pushes it out. It pushes the inner pad onto the disc. What to check: Moves freely. Not seized, rusty or leaking.

Piston seal

A rubber ring. It stops fluid leaking out, and pulls the piston back a little when you let go. What to check: No fluid leaking past the piston.

Caliper slide pins

Let the caliper slide in and out so both pads press evenly. What to check: Slide freely, greased, and rubber boots not split.

Brake pads

Squeeze the disc. The friction between pad and disc slows the wheel. What to check: Enough friction material left (usually at least 3 mm – check the data). No cracks. Even wear.

Brake disc (rotor)

Turns with the wheel. The pads squeeze it to slow the car. What to check: Smooth surface. No deep scores, cracks or big lip on the edge.

Caliper carrier

Bolted to the car. Holds the pads and the slide pins. What to check: Bolts tight. Pads slide in it freely.

Bleed nipple

A small valve used to let air out of the brake fluid. This is called bleeding the brakes. What to check: Not seized, and the dust cap is fitted.
